One stormy evening on the coast of the Great Lakes, a lone keeper named Jack found himself lost in the dense fog of the lake. Jack, a grizzled sailor with a keen eye for detail, had spent his life tending to the lighthouse he built to guide lost sailors. Every night, the lighthouses beacon, a small bellows, would jingle steadily even when Jack himself was not present. This became a nostalgic routine he knew all too well.

One late night, Jack, feeling the misty atmosphere and missing air-conditioned comfort, decided to leave his usual routine. He went out into the dark and fog with only his eyes telling him where things were and where he was. Jack followed the familiar trail, his eyes trained to watch for the beam of the lighthouse as it cut through the air.

Suddenly, he saw something unusual, the light of the fog had dipped and a narrow beam pierced through. Jack stood frozen, for now the beam had reached him. Suddenly, the glass broke away, a small bottle lay on Jack's feet. Jack took the bottle out of his pocket and rummaged through his bag, and there he found a mysterious note. 

"A message in a bottle," was the note, and more importantly, the message inside was a letter from the lighthouse keeper of many years past, "Rosebud, your father." 

Jack opened the letter and read the words, "Rosebud, my father... I left this world a lot earlier than I would have. I will miss you. I wrote this letter and hope it didn't take forever to make... Please read this and see, but my tears will still echo in the fog."
